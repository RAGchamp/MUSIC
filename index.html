What began as a small initiative to explore AI's ability to work out the notes of a song has, within a week, grown into several workstreams: practising, learning, playing and creating music. This is a human-led, AI-assisted initiative, and it shows how AI helps people test their ideas faster. In the pre-AI world, use cases like these could easily have taken six months or more to assess; with AI they were done within a week. And this is just the beginning. No sign-up, nothing to install: it all runs in your browser, on a laptop or a phone.

The vision has not changed

Why a music app? To learn how to use AI well.

RAG Champ explores what AI can really do, and shows tech professionals how to become AI amplifiers. Music is the latest experiment, not a change of direction.

An AI amplifier is a professional who can guide an organisation to make optimal use of AI: getting high-quality output with the fewest resources in the shortest time.
🏆
High-quality outputEvery note list is checked against the original recording; every page is tested in a headless browser.
🪶
Fewest resourcesPlain HTML files with no build step, no server and no database. Built on an ordinary laptop.
⚡
Shortest time13 interactive pages in one week, from requests written in everyday language and screenshots.

1 · Where it started

🎶 Practise songs with the original recording

The first use case, and still the heart of the app.

🎸🎹🪗

Song Practice

Pick a song and press Play. The original recording plays while each note lights up in yellow on a piano keyboard, a guitar fretboard and tab, or an accordion, along with the note boxes.

  • Five film songs: Inteha Ho Gayi, Tip Tip Barsa Pani, Pehla Nasha, Chura Liya Hai, Anhoni Ko Honi
  • Slow down to 50 % without changing the pitch, loop, and tap any note to hear it on its own
  • On a phone the fretboard stands upright so it fits the screen
▶ Open Song Practice
🤖

How AI found the notes

The timings of every note were found by Python code that listens to the recording (pitch, note starts, tuning) and were then checked by ear.

2 · Play

🎹 Pianos you can watch, and play

🙌

Piano player with animated hands

Two hands with numbered fingers press the keys while the score scrolls along. Switch to a falling notes view, play only a range of bars, and change the speed.

  • Für Elise: the whole piece, read from a PDF entirely by code
  • Pehla Nasha: a 5-minute, 177-bar arrangement read from screenshots
▶ Try the piano player
📱

Mini Piano

One octave of big keys made for phones.

  • Manual: tap (several fingers at once) or use the laptop keys
  • Automated: a table of keys and waits that plays back a tune
  • ✨ Highlight keys: the next key glows to teach you a tune, with a counter for repeated notes

5 · Behind the scenes

🔬 The tools AI built to check its own work

Getting the notes right needed tools. These are the ones Claude Code built to measure and check its results, the part that turns AI output from "looks right" into "is right".

🧪
Notes TesterPlays a song's notes on real instrument samples and compares them with the original recording, line by line, with suggested fixes.
🔬
WAV VisualizerThe notes found in two recordings, drawn side by side as coloured boxes, with the differences outlined.
⌨️
Piano CapturePlay along with a recording on an 88-key piano; your key strokes become timed note lines.
🎙️
RecorderRecords another browser tab's audio as a WAV file, and trims a clip.
🙌
Piano Notes PlayerLoad any notes file and watch it played with fingering and a scrolling score.

6 · Human + AI

🤝 What I did, and what Claude Code did

I did not hand-write the code. I described what I wanted in plain words, often with a screenshot, then listened, tested and said what to change. This split is the core lesson for an AI amplifier.

🧑 What I did: direction and judgement

  • Chose the songs, recorded the clips and found the sheet music
  • Described each feature in everyday language
  • Listened: "it sounds like a spoon on a steel bowl", "the timing is off"
  • Decided the look and feel, and when to keep things simple
  • Tested every page and asked for fixes

🤖 What Claude Code did: the building

  • Wrote every HTML, CSS and JavaScript page
  • Analysed recordings with Python to find the notes and their timings
  • Read sheet music from PDFs and screenshots into notes
  • Built its own test tools and checked pages in a headless browser
  • Wrote the "How it works" guides
