Suno Guide: Making Songs That Sound Planned

Suno turns a style description and your lyrics into a finished song, with voice and band. Typing “a happy Hindi song” gives you a song; this guide shows how to get your song: the right genre, tempo, instruments, voice, structure and length, then how to fix and finish it.

1. How Suno Works

Two inputs in, two songs out
Style how it sounds: genre, era, tempo, instruments, voice, mood
Lyrics what is sung, and in what order: words plus section tags like [Chorus]
Create each press makes two different versions from the same input
Random the same input gives new songs every time: it is a dice roll, not a recipe

Suno doesn't read sheet music or follow exact seconds. It reads your words the way a session band reads a brief from a producer: it gets the feel right easily and the details (exact intro length, exact melody) only roughly. So the skill is in (a) writing a clear brief, (b) generating several takes, and (c) keeping the best parts and fixing the rest.

2. Getting Started: Simple vs Custom

  1. Sign in at suno.com (Google, Microsoft, Apple or Discord account) and open Create.
  2. Pick the model from the drop-down at the top. Newer models sound clearer and follow instructions better; the newest are usually for paid plans.
  3. Choose the mode:
ModeYou giveUse it for
SimpleOne sentence, e.g. “a fast Hindi dance song about a village girl”Quick ideas. Suno writes the lyrics itself, and you control very little.
CustomYour own Lyrics, a Style box, a Title, and advanced optionsEvery real song. The rest of this guide is about Custom mode.

Useful switches in Custom mode (names can change between versions):

  • Instrumental: no vocals; the lyrics box is ignored except for tags.
  • Exclude styles: things you don't want, e.g. autotune, female vocals, rock guitar.
  • Advanced options (paid models): sliders such as Weirdness (how experimental) and Style influence (how strictly it follows your style text), and a vocal gender choice.
  • Persona: save a voice and style from a song you liked, to reuse it for the next song, so an album keeps one singer.
  • Upload audio: start from your own recording (a hummed tune, a guitar riff) and let Suno extend or cover it.

3. Writing the Style Prompt

Formula: genre + era/region · tempo · key/mood · instruments · voice · production

Put the most important words first; Suno gives the start of the style box the most weight. Use short comma-separated phrases, not long sentences.

PartExamples
GenreRaï-popBollywood danceghazallo-fi hip hopPunjabi bhangra
Era / region90sNorth Africanretro 80s Bollywood

Instrumentssynth accordion leaddarbukadholtablasitarbansuri flute

✗ Vague

nice Hindi song, good music, like Khaled

No tempo, no instruments, no voice. And it names a real artist: Suno blocks or ignores artist names.

✓ Clear

90s Algerian Raï-pop, Bollywood fusion, 128 BPM, A minor, Hijaz scale, synth accordion lead, darbuka and dhol, handclaps, off-beat synth bass, energetic male Hindi vocals, melismatic, group chants

Describes the sound of the artist instead of naming him.

Tip: 8–15 well-chosen phrases work better than 40. If two words fight (e.g. “calm, aggressive”), Suno picks one at random. Use Exclude styles to push away what keeps creeping in.

4. Writing Lyrics with Tags

Tags in square brackets tell Suno what each part is. They are not sung. Put each tag on its own line, before the words of that part.

Section tags: [Intro][Verse 1][Pre-Chorus][Chorus] [Post-Chorus][Bridge][Instrumental][Break] [Solo][Drop][Outro][End]

Descriptive tags (a section name plus a hint): [Intro: synth accordion riff, darbuka][Bridge: soft, no drums] [Chorus: full band, crowd shouts][Male vocal][Whispered]

You writeWhat Suno does
(Hey! Hey!)Words in round brackets are usually sung as backing vocals or echoes.
RANI!CAPITALS often get sung louder or shouted.
Raaa-niStretched vowels and hyphens make a long, held note.
Short lines, 6–10 syllablesEasier to fit the beat. Very long lines get rushed or cut.
Same line count in each verseVerses sound like a pair and keep the same melody.
Repeat the chorus text fullyWriting it out each time (not “Chorus ×2”) keeps it the same each time.

5. Controlling Structure and Length

Suno thinks in sections, not seconds

You can't type “intro = 15 seconds”. But you can get close, because time = bars ÷ tempo. At 128 BPM a bar of 4 beats lasts 1.875 s, so a 15-second intro is 8 bars. Write the length into the tag and the style:

[Intro: 8 bars instrumental, synth accordion riff, darbuka builds, crowd shout "Hey!"]
  • Song length comes from the amount of lyrics and the number of sections. For ~3 minutes at a fast tempo: intro, 3 choruses, 2 verses, a break, a bridge and an outro is about right.
  • Instrumental parts: use [Instrumental] or [Solo: accordion] with no words under it.
  • Clean ending: finish with [Outro] and then [End], otherwise songs can fade or stop mid-phrase.
  • Too short? Use Extend from a chosen second, with the rest of the lyrics. Too long? Crop it in the song editor.
  • Check the length afterwards: listen with a timer, or load the downloaded WAV into a tool like the WAV Visualizer.

6. Hindi and Indian Songs

  • Say the language in the style box: Hindi vocals (or Punjabi, Urdu). Otherwise Roman-script Hindi may be sung with an English accent.
  • Roman script or Devanagari? Try both on a short test. Devanagari (रानी) often gives better pronunciation; Roman (Rani) is easier to edit. Stay with one for a whole song.
  • Spell for sound: if a word comes out wrong, re-spell it the way it should sound: qurbaan → kur-baan, dil → dill.
  • Name Indian instruments exactly: tabla, dhol, dholak, sitar, sarangi, shehnai, bansuri, harmonium, santoor.
  • Style words that work: Bollywood, filmi, qawwali, ghazal, bhangra, Sufi, 90s Bollywood romantic, Indian classical fusion.
  • Ornaments: melismatic, murki, alaap intro push the singer towards Indian-style decorations.
Hindi pronunciation is the most common problem. Make a 30-second test with just the chorus first, fix the spellings, and only then make the full song.

8. A Workflow That Saves Credits

Plan on paper, test small, then build
1PlanWrite the song map: sections, bars, tempo, instruments (like the Rani Rani logic page).
2Test the soundStyle box + only the chorus. Make 2–3 rounds until the sound and voice are right.
3Full songSame style, all lyrics with tags. Make 2–4 rounds (4–8 versions).
4PickChoose the best whole take, not the best single moment.
5FixReplace bad sections, extend, crop.
6FinishSave a Persona, download WAV + stems, name the version.
  • Change one thing at a time between rounds, so you know what helped.
  • Keep a log (a text file): style text, lyrics version and what you liked. Good prompts are worth keeping.
  • Like or keep good takes right away; the library fills up fast.

9. Troubleshooting

ProblemTry
Wrong genre or instrumentsMove the genre to the start of the style; add the unwanted ones to Exclude styles; raise Style influence.
Tags are sung as wordsPut each tag alone on its own line in square brackets; keep tag hints short.
Intro too short or vocals start at onceA descriptive intro tag with bars (section 5), plus long instrumental intro in the style.
Song ends abruptly or fades oddlyAdd [Outro] and [End]; or Extend and then Crop.
Words mispronouncedRe-spell by sound, try Devanagari, add the language to the style (section 6); fix just that line with Replace section.
Words rushed or skippedShorter lines, fewer lines per section, a slower tempo.
Voice changes between songsMake a Persona from the take you like.
Sounds muddy or harshTry a newer model, Remaster, or add clean mix, clear vocals to the style.
Prompt refusedRemove real artist names and copyrighted lyrics; describe the style instead.

10. Credits, Plans and Rights

Each Create uses credits (two versions per press). As of late 2026 the plans look roughly like this; prices, models and limits change often, so check suno.com/pricing before you rely on them.

PlanCreditsNotes
Free50 a day (about 10 songs)Older model; no commercial use; downloads are now restricted.
Pro (~$10/month)2,500 a monthNewest models, commercial use of songs made while subscribed, WAV and stems.
Premier (~$30/month)10,000 a monthAs Pro, plus Suno Studio. Unused credits do not roll over.
  • Own lyrics stay yours. Use your own words, like the Rani Rani lyrics. Never paste another song's lyrics.
  • Publishing (YouTube, Spotify) needs a paid plan for the song's creation; say it is AI-assisted where the platform asks.
  • Inspired by, not copied from: describe a genre (Raï-pop) rather than a song (Didi), so the result is your own.

11. Worked Example: Rani Rani

Everything from the Rani Rani logic page, ready to paste into Custom mode

Title: Rani Rani

Style:

90s Algerian Raï-pop, Bollywood fusion, 128 BPM, A minor, Hijaz scale, synth accordion lead, shehnai, darbuka and dhol, tabla fills, handclaps, off-beat synth bass, brass stabs, energetic male Hindi vocals, melismatic, group chants, long instrumental intro

Exclude styles:

female vocals, rock guitar, autotune, slow ballad

Lyrics:

Hey!) (Hey!) [Chorus: full band] Rani, Rani, o meri Rani Teri aankhon mein jaadu ki kahani Rani, Rani, na kar manmaani Dil le gayi tu, o meri deewani (Hey! Hey!) [Instrumental: 8 bars synth accordion solo, dhol, claps, crowd shouts "RANI!"] [Verse 2: brass stabs] Mehndi wale haath tere, choodiyon ki jhankaar Hans ke jo tu chal de, to ruk jaaye bazaar Maa se poochha, baap se poochha, poochha saara gaon Sab ne kaha "woh Rani hai", le le uska naam Raat jagi hai, taare jaage, jaaga hai yeh pyaar Ek baar tu haan kar de, bas ek hi baar Haan haan, bas ek hi baar Aaja, aaja, naach le zara!
