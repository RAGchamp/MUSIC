1. Adapted from “Mehbooba” and Boney M

Two 1970s hits, one campfire

“Mehbooba Mehbooba” plays in Sholay (1975) while a dancer performs at the bandit Gabbar Singh's camp. R.D. Burman sang it himself in a rough, smoky voice over a swaying Middle-Eastern groove; it was his own adaptation of Demis Roussos's “Say You Love Me” (1974), so borrowing a style is part of its story too.

Boney M's “Daddy Cool” (1976) set the Euro-disco formula: an octave-jumping bass, a deep male voice that half sings, half speaks, and a high female trio that answers and repeats the hook. Boney M also loved story songs about one exotic character, so a desert dancer called Laila fits them well.

What comes from where

What was not taken

  • No lyrics from “Mehbooba”, “Daddy Cool” or any other song: every line is new.
  • No melody: the hook riff and the vocal tune are new; only the scale, the groove and the way the voices share the song are reused.
  • A new name: the hook is “Laila”, not “Mehbooba”.

The story in short

Bandits sit round a fire in a desert canyon. A mysterious dancer with a silver bell appears, and every rough man forgets his gun. The chief offers her his horse; she laughs and keeps dancing. By morning she's gone, and only her footprints are left, waiting for the night she comes back. Like a Boney M legend, nobody knows who she is.

6. Music Description

Part of the bandWhat it plays
BassThe heart of the Boney M sound: octave-jumping eighth notes (D3–D2), with E♭ as a passing note
DrumsDisco four-on-the-floor kick, open hi-hat on the off-beats, claps on 2 and 4, darbuka accents, a galloping tom in the intro and outro
LeadBouzouki hook riff; nylon-string Spanish guitar flourishes as in “Mehbooba”
StringsSweeping disco strings in the choruses, an upward sweep into each section
VocalsDeep, almost spoken male storyteller; bright, tight female trio for echoes and the hook; breathy rhythm grunts on the off-beats
EffectsDesert wind, hooves, ankle bells (ghungroo), a gunshot echo at the very end

Energy curve

  • Cinematic: intro, wind and hooves before the beat
  • Groovy: verses, the storyteller and the echoes
  • First peak: the bouzouki and guitar solo
  • Tense: breakdown, drums and bass, whispers
  • Biggest: final chorus and the “Lai-la! Hey! Ho!” chant
  • Fade: the desert takes her back
